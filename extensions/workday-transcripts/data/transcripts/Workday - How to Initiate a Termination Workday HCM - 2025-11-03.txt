Welcome to Workday HCM termination. In this session, we'll review the termination business process and learn how to initiate a termination. We'll also discuss job overlaps when hiring and terminating.

Termination ends the active working relationship between an enterprise and an employee. You can initiate a termination with the termination business process. This business process captures important data and steps such as termination reasons, equipment inventory, and more. The termination process is configurable. You can indicate if the employee is eligible for rehire and whether the termination is regrettable. You can also configure notice periods and attach documents. Additionally, you can include questionnaires to record information such as exit interviews.

When terminating an employee, you must specify the termination date, which is based on the employee's time zone. The employee's last day of work cannot occur after their termination date. Individuals will remain in the system as active employees up until midnight on the day of the termination.

Terminations can occur for many different reasons. It's important to track and report on this data to understand turnover rates. Therefore, you can configure more than one termination reason for a single termination event on the termination and end contingent worker business processes. In this example, all fields with red asterisks are required fields, including the primary reason field.

You can use the maintain termination categories task to group individual reasons into reason categories. Categories can include involuntary reasons, retirement reasons, resignation reasons, and more. These categories and reasons will display when initiating a termination. Additionally, you can configure termination reasons to meet reporting goals by location. Use the maintain local termination reasons task to create termination reasons that reflect local requirements of a country or region. You can also require the termination reason field by location using the maintain localization settings task. For example, you can require the field for terminated workers in Argentina but designate the same field as optional for US workers.

Now let's discuss how to initiate the termination business process. Managers, administrators, and other users with security permissions can initiate an employee termination by using the terminate employee task. However, if your organization enables the submit resignation business process, workers can initiate their own resignation event from the actions button on their worker profile.

When you initiate a termination, the system requires specific data to process the termination. You enter this data using the guided editor framework. Let's explore an example. Brian Sullivan, the HR partner for the marketing services group, needs to terminate Jason Bole. To begin, Brian will navigate to Jason's worker profile and select the actions button. From the menu, he'll select job change and then terminate employee.

From the guided editor, Brian will enter the primary termination reason, the termination date, and other important details. Optionally, he configures a notice period; however, this section only displays if you enable notice periods for the location country where the employee works. In the regrettable section, Brian indicates that the termination is a loss to the organization and determines that the employee is eligible for rehire at a future date. Lastly, Brian can indicate whether to close the position. He might consider this option due to downsizing or a reorganization. However, this is a voluntary termination, so he will keep the position open. He can also specify the position for job overlap and attach any files or comments to the termination process.

Once Brian submits this task, any additional approvals and steps awaiting action, like completing the questionnaire, will need to be completed before the termination takes effect. These workflow steps will vary depending on your business process configuration. Therefore, it's important to familiarize yourself with your organization's process before initiating a termination.

Job overlap functionality allows two workers to occupy the same position for a limited period of time. This is useful for training a replacement for a worker leaving their position or backfilling a position while a worker takes a leave of absence. When initiating a termination, you have the option to designate the position for job overlap. You can also activate job overlaps when hiring or make specific positions eligible for overlap even before a job vacancy date exists.

To identify the new worker from the existing worker, Workday identifies the new hire as the overlapping worker and the original worker as the primary worker. It's important to note that overlap workers share all security roles associated with the position restrictions during their tenure in the overlap position, including managers. Also, all business processes will route to both workers, and both workers will display when previewing the organization. Additionally, Workday does not synchronize organization assignments across positions and position restrictions. Any organization changes will only apply to the position you take action on.

You can track positions with overlap using the overlap workers and positions with overlap reports. In this session, we learned how to terminate using the termination business process and how to configure a job overlap. Thank you for taking the time to learn with Workday.
